NASCAR Joins IndyCar for a Rare Doubleheader Skip to Content Jonathan Bachman // Getty Images Open-wheel racing is heading back to the desert. Phoenix Raceway announced the NTT IndyCar Series will return to the one-mile oval on Saturday, March 7, 2026, as part of a doubleheader with the NASCAR O’Reilly Auto Parts Series. It’s the first IndyCar stop in the Valley of the Sun since 2018 and a continuation of a history that stretches back to 1964. Related Story F1 Shakes Up Sprint Locations for 2026 For fans, it means one thing: a weekend stacked with variety. Saturday will showcase the open-wheel machines of IndyCar, while Sunday will feature the rubbin’-is-racin’ stock cars of the NASCAR Cup Series. What It Means for IndyCar The Phoenix return plugs a gap in IndyCar’s western footprint. The series has struggled to maintain a consistent oval presence outside Indianapolis, with Phoenix often viewed as a proving ground for both rookies and veterans. Jake Galstad // Getty Images Jonathan Bachman // Getty Images The return bolsters IndyCar’s schedule with a race that blends history, market potential, and a chance to showcase its stars on short-oval turf. It’s also a signal that the series isn’t content to cede major markets to NASCAR and Formula 1. What It Means for NASCAR For NASCAR, sharing the stage isn’t a threat—it’s an opportunity. The O’Reilly Auto Parts Series (NASCAR’s secondary national series) gets a bump from crossover fans, and the Cup Series benefits from a weekend that could swell crowds and TV eyeballs. Related Stories Autoweek Racing on TV This Week: Sept. 15-21 They Call Him Butterbean NASCAR has been experimenting with cross-discipline weekends (see IMSA at Daytona or Trucks with SRX tie-ins), but a true IndyCar/NASCAR doubleheader is rare territory, underscoring Phoenix’s growing role as a marquee track. Since 2020, Phoenix has been the home of NASCAR’s Championship Weekend, and its reputation as a fan-friendly, action-heavy venue has only grown. Adding IndyCar back into the mix not only honors the oval’s open-wheel history but also makes the track one of the few venues in the world hosting both top-flight NASCAR and IndyCar on the same weekend. It’s a coup for the track and a win for motorsports fans in the Southwest. The broader significance? A motorsports calendar where series overlap more strategically, serving fans who crave diversity in what—and how—they watch.
