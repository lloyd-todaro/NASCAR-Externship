Zilisch’s Xfinity Win at COTA Proves He’s the Future of NASCAR Skip to Content James Gilbert // Getty Images Anyone doubting whether Connor Zilisch is indeed the next big thing in NASCAR needed only to spend a couple of hours watching him at work in the Xfinity Series race at the Circuit of the Americas near Austin, Texas on Saturday. The 18-year-old delivered a performance so electrifying, so utterly defiant of adversity, that by the time he took the checkered flag, any remaining skeptics had likely piled up their doubts and set them on fire. Zilisch started from pole and led a race-high 26 of the 65 laps, but don’t mistake that for a leisurely Saturday afternoon drive. This was no effortless cruise through the Texas countryside. This was a battle—one filled with mistakes, misfortune, and a dash of chaos that somehow made his victory all the more impressive. Things began to unravel on lap 19 when Zilisch was caught in one of those cruel, split-second moments that make racing the heartbreaker it is. Just as he was entering the pits, the caution flag flew, and in a case of absolutely rotten timing, officials closed the pits a mere breath before he crossed the line. The penalty? A one-way trip to the back of the field. Most drivers would have seen their race effectively ruined. Zilisch, however, is not most drivers. Amazing 18-Year-Old Zilisch Wins Xfinity Debut JR Motorsports Signs 17-year-old to Xfinity Deal He began a furious charge back through the pack, carving his way forward with the precision of a surgeon and the aggression of a man who simply refuses to lose. And just when it seemed like his comeback story was building to a Hollywood ending, he found himself in yet another crisis. Contact with Corey Heim left Zilisch’s JR Motorsports Chevrolet sporting a right front tire that looked about as stable as a house of cards in a hurricane. Smoke billowed. Disaster loomed. Surely, this would be the moment that broke him. It wasn’t. Instead, Zilisch shook off the adversity yet again, found his way back to the front, and took the lead for good with nine laps remaining. That, however, was hardly the end of the drama. Behind him, his JR Motorsports teammate Carson Kvapil—desperate for his first career win—threw everything he had at the race leader. But unlike Zilisch, Kvapil’s luck ran out. A punctured right front tire with five laps to go ended his challenge and left Zilisch with just one more problem to manage: fuel. With William Byron in his mirrors, Zilisch had to strike the delicate balance of maintaining pace while keeping enough gas in the tank to make it home. Byron, fresh off winning the first stage, did his best to reel him in, slicing a four-second gap down to 1.6, but he simply ran out of time. Zilisch crossed the line, victorious, capturing his second career Xfinity win in a performance that proved once and for all that the hype is very, very real. “That was way harder than I wanted it to be,” Zilisch admitted after the race. “I did not drive a clean race at all … I was kind of mentally fogged halfway through that race. I made way too many mistakes … I was glad we were able to fight back.” Byron, meanwhile, had to settle for second. “I had fun. It was a good workout,” he said. “It was good to get back into the rhythm of things, pushing the car. I think it’ll help me for (Sunday).” Sam Mayer, Austin Hill, and Christian Eckes rounded out the top five. And Zilisch? He gets to do it all over again on Sunday, making his NASCAR Cup Series debut from the 14th starting spot. But if Saturday proved anything, it’s that starting 14th is about as concerning for him as a mosquito bite. Because when the green flag drops, the kid knows how to find the front. “It’s been a fun weekend, but we’ve got a big race tomorrow,” he said. “I’m looking forward to that as well.” So are we, Connor.
