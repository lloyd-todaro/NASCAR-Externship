Tilton / Getty Images The Breakfast Club podcast welcomed NASCAR icon Bubba Wallace to a recent episode where the 23XI racer discussed the personalities of drivers in the sport. Describing himself as one of the more likeable and authentic personalities on the grid, he admits he's always tried to be "super relatable." Wallace has gained momentum in the 2025 Cup Series, finishing third at both Martinsville and Homestead, and is looking as though he could bring his long drought to an end sooner rather than later. Talking to The Breakfast Club , the driver discussed what he sees as a lack of personalities across the grid, after years of building a reputation of talking his mind. "We look at the sport and the personalities that you have inside our sport and there's a select few and I feel like I'm near the top of the list of just being personable and likable despite all the BS that goes on. "I'm going to tell you how I feel, and I feel like a lot of us inside our sport are very straight edged and cookie cutter," Wallace said. "And that's OK. You don't want to show your true colours or show your cards, you keep that in your personal life, that's fine." Bubba Wallace, 23XI Racing Toyota Photo by: Jared C. Tilton/Getty Images He continued, adding that building a personality is important to building sponsor relationships. "But for me, I've always tried to be super relatable to everyone I meet and make a lasting impression to eventually have the investments, and the funding come in. It seemed like nothing we ever did would work and for whatever reason that is, it was what it was, but it never stopped me from pursuing what I wanted to do and fighting hard. "Now, I look at the people who potentially said, 'no, we're good,' and just laugh because we're in a good spot now." Wallace's place in NASCAR hasn't always been smooth sailing, with him often fighting against the grain. In 2020, he led the successful campaign to ban Confederate flags at NASCAR events. This saw plenty of backlash from traditionalists. "I went from being one of the most - I wouldn't say the most liked drivers - but favourable drivers. But now it's just been the boos and stuff. And that's okay because it's sports … Booing is a part of sports in general. But the next week after Talladega, it was like a light switch," he admitted.
