Christopher Bell Is First to Two Wins in NASCAR Cup Season After COTA Triumph Skip to Content Icon Sportswire // Getty Images After snatching the lead from Kyle Busch with six laps remaining, Christopher Bell held off a hard-charging William Byron to claim the first back-to-back victories of his career. Coupled with Bell’s victory a week ago at Atlanta Motor Speedway, the Joe Gibbs Racing driver became the first driver to win two of the season’s first three races since 2018. Kevin Harvick accomplished the feat that year, winning the season’s second race at Atlanta and the third at Las Vegas. “Whenever Kyle was leading, I was just trying to be cautious,” Bell said after recording the 11th victory of his NASCAR Cup career. “I wanted to pass him clean. He could get off the corners just good enough that I couldn’t get inside of him. But then I started peeking a nose, and he bobbled and allowed me to get out front. Whenever I did (get out front), I’m like, OK, just don’t beat yourself. Those were about the five or six sloppiest laps I’ve ever run.” Bell edged Byron by 0.433 second for his second victory this year, meaning he has now won more than one race in a season for four straight years. An exhausted Byron said he was sitting in third when Busch and Bell were fighting for the lead, waiting for one of them to bobble or slide their tires. After Bell passed Busch, Byron noted that Bell’s Toyota was “super loose”. “It kind of gave me a couple of shots at him. I just couldn’t ever get beside him,” Byron said. “Just sucks to be so damn close. You can be on the bumper of the guy coming to the line, and that sucks.” Icon Sportswire // Getty Images Chase Elliott (9) get turned around early at COTA. Elliott Rebounds From Disastrous First Lap An aggressive move by Ross Chastain on the EchoPark Automotive Grand Prix’s opening lap sent Chase Elliott spinning. The accident that left Elliott’s Hendrick Motorsports Chevrolet with a broken toe link, also damaged Denny Hamlin’s Toyota and Connor Zilisch’s Chevrolet. On lap six of the 95-lap race, Elliott was 29th. He advanced through the field as his fellow competitors pitted. By lap 18, the Georgia native was seventh and he ended the first Stage on lap 20 in fifth. However, the rest of Elliott’s race resembled a yo-yo as his crew took advantage of the first caution period to repair the Hendrick Motorsports Chevrolet’s toe link. When the race’s second Stage went green on lap 26, Elliott was 36th in the 37-car field. By lap 46, Elliott had made it back to 15th; however, pit stops during the second caution period dropped him back through the field and on lap 50, he was 31st. Elliott once again began maneuvering through the field. By lap 55, he was 26th. He pitted for tires during the fourth and final caution period that consumed laps 79-82 and restarted 25th. With 10 laps remaining in the race, Elliott mounted a charge from 11th. He was fifth with two laps to go on the 2.4-mile course. Elliott passed the fourth-place Kyle Busch to claim that position. “It was a crazy day,” Elliott said. “We got behind on a restart there and just had to play major catchup. “(Crew chief) Alan Gustafson made a great call there at the end to put tires on it. We were rolling up through there really good at the end. Obviously, when you have a good car like that, I would have liked to have been in the fight with those guys, but it was a great recovery from where we were at during the end of the second stage.” Icon Sportswire // Getty Images Kyle Busch (8) was good, but not good enough, on Sunday. Busch Top Lap Leader, But No Victory Kyle Busch led six times for 42 laps in the 92-lap EchoPark Automotive Grand Prix and appeared poised to snap his 59-race winless streak. However, a torrid duel with eventual winner Christopher Bell in the closing laps wore out his right-rear tire and Busch had to settle for fifth. “I just wish that maybe we had equal tires to the No. 20 (winner Christopher Bell),” Busch said. “I don’t know if that was all the difference. Even that last yellow flag that we had, I was far enough out front that I could run the clean lines, the lines that I wanted, to preserve the tires and take care of them as much as I could to see if he could get there.” However, the final caution period that consumed laps 79-82 left Busch in defensive mode. “You’re in complete and utter just beat the heck out of the tires at that point, and I just didn’t have it over the No. 20 (Bell),” Busch said. “Hate it that the contact that we made between (turns) two and three ruined our race car, too. It bent the right-rear toe link and knocked everything out of it. I just didn’t have anything there at the end to compete with those guys.” James Gilbert // Getty Images Connor Zilisch had a rough ending to his day in Texas. Wreck Foils Zilisch’s Cup Debut One day after Connor Zilisch won NASCAR’s Xfinity Series race at Circuit of the Americas the 18-year-old driver made his Cup debut. Even though his Trackhouse Racing Chevrolet suffered damage in an opening lap accident involving Chase Elliott, Denny Hamlin and Ross Chastain, he continued and had cracked the top 15 with 50 laps remaining in the 95-lap race. However, on lap 50 in turn 19, Daniel Suarez spun, and Zilisch slammed into the right front of his teammate’s Chevrolet. Both were eliminated from the event. Suarez was credited with a 36th-place finish and Zilisch with last in the 37-car field. “All I saw was a cloud of smoke, and by the time I saw him (Suarez), it was way too late to do anything,” Zilisch said. “I saw him spinning off to the left and I thought he was going to keep going in that direction or stay there. I guess he flipped back right, and he started coming towards me.” Zilisch described the end to his Cup debut as “unfortunate.” “We were one of the top five fastest cars in the second Stage,” Zilisch said. “I went from outside the top 30 to 14th, and I felt really good about our Chevy. We made a lot of gains from practice and qualifying.” Suarez said when he hit the curb and then the ground it spun him. “When you have low air pressure, that’s very easy to do because the car is lower to the ground,” Suarez said. “I wish I could say it was a big mistake, but it wasn’t.
